# Intelligence Profiling Tradecraft & Methodology

This document outlines the structured intelligence methodologies, analytical frameworks, and OSINT collection standards utilized by premier intelligence agencies (CIA, MI6, FSB, DGSE, Mossad) adapted for open-source persona and target profiling.

---

## 1. The Intelligence Cycle for Target Profiling

Profiling follows the strict 5-stage Intelligence Cycle to avoid bias, premature closure, and superficial synthesis:

```
[1. Planning & Direction (PIRs)]
              ↓
  [2. Multi-Vector OSINT Collection]
              ↓
   [3. Processing & Source Grading]
              ↓
[4. Analysis & Behavioral Assessment]
              ↓
[5. Dossier Production & Dissemination]
```

### Phase 1: Planning & Direction (Priority Intelligence Requirements - PIRs)
Before collecting any data, the analyst must state:
1. **Temporal Baseline Anchor**: Record execution date (`YYYY-MM-DD`). Actively query the sitting Head of State and statutory cabinet authorities presiding at this exact date. Never infer active incumbency from past articles or training priors.
2. **Target Identification**: Full legal name, aliases, official capacity, jurisdiction, and active tenure dates (`[YYYY–Present]` vs `[YYYY–YYYY]`).
3. **Profile Purpose**: Why is the target being profiled? (e.g., judicial posture, negotiation prep, risk assessment, leadership transition).
4. **PIR Definition**:
   - **PIR-1 (Trajectory & Patrons)**: What was their career escalator? Who sponsored or appointed them?
   - **PIR-2 (Operating Doctrine)**: What are their observable decision-making patterns and jurisprudential/operational philosophy?
   - **PIR-3 (Network & Affiliations)**: Who belongs to their inner circle, institutional bloc, or adversarial axis? Annotate all nodes with active vs. past tenure relative to today's date.
   - **PIR-4 (Vulnerabilities & Friction)**: What controversies, overturned decisions, conflicts of interest, or ethical complaints exist?

## 2. Multi-Vector OSINT Collection Matrix

Collection strictly relies on publicly available, high-trust open sources across five orthogonal vectors:

| Vector | Source Categories | Priority Target Data |
|---|---|---|
| **V1: Institutional & State Records** | Official Gazettes (Diario Oficial), Transparency Portals, Ministry decrees, Judicial branches, Electoral registries, Declarations of Assets and Interests (InfoProbidad / patrimonial disclosures). | Exact appointment dates, salary, declared properties/assets, past state positions, administrative sanctions. |
| **V2: Judicial & Jurisprudential Dockets** | Court dockets, appellate rulings, public hearing records, Supreme Court nomination hearings, bar association files. | Litigative win/loss record, frequency of preventive detention requests, procedural rigor, known doctrinal interpretations. |
| **V3: Academic & Intellectual Footprint** | University faculty pages, law review papers, master/doctoral theses, symposium presentations, academic citations. | Doctrinal orientation (e.g., penal dogmatics, restorative justice, punitive efficiency), intellectual mentors, philosophical posture. |
| **V4: Press & Media Archive** | National & regional newspaper archives, investigative journalism repositories, broadcast interviews, press conferences. | Rhetorical style, media savvy, response to controversy, public self-image, notable quotes. |
| **V5: Network & Institutional Affiliations** | Bar associations, prosecutors' associations, university alumni networks, academic boards, corporate registries. | Institutional loyalties, committee memberships, factional alignment within the institution. |

## 3. Information Evaluation (Admiralty Code / NATO STANAG 2022)

Every key fact in the dossier must be evaluated using the Admiralty Code:

### Source Reliability
- **A — Completely reliable**: Official government portals, gazettes, certified state registries.
- **B — Usually reliable**: Established investigative journalism with named on-record sources, university faculty publications.
- **C — Fairly reliable**: General press reports, professional association publications.
- **D — Not usually reliable**: Social media speculation, anonymous blogs, unverified leaks.
- **E — Unreliable**: Hostile partisan sources, contradicted assertions.
- **F — Reliability cannot be judged**: Untested sources.

### Information Credibility
- **1 — Confirmed by other sources**: Fact corroborated by 2+ independent primary vectors.
- **2 — Probably true**: Strongly supported by circumstantial institutional evidence.
- **3 — Possibly true**: Plausible assertion, but lacks multi-vector verification.
- **4 — Doubtful**: Inconsistent with known career facts or contradictory.
- **5 — Improbable**: Contradicted by official records.
- **6 — Truth cannot be judged**: Insufficient context.

*Standard*: Only findings rated **A1 through B2** may be presented as verified facts. Ratings of **B3/C3** must be explicitly qualified as "Uncorroborated report" or "Secondary media claim".

## 4. The CIA "Assessment of Persons" Behavioral Framework

Adapted from the CIA Center for the Analysis of Personality and Political Behavior (pioneered by Dr. Jerrold Post), every target is assessed across four psychological/behavioral dimensions:

### A. Cognitive & Decision-Making Style
- **Proceduralist vs. Substantive**: Do they prioritize strict formal adherence to the letter of the law/procedure, or are they focused on tangible outcomes and moral imperatives?
- **Risk Profile**: Are they cautious and risk-averse (seeking institutional consensus) or aggressive and bold (willing to pioneer risky indictments or novel legal interpretations)?
- **Analytical Depth**: Do they operate on granular technical mastery, or do they delegate heavily to specialized teams?

### B. Motivational Drivers & Ambition (SMICE Matrix)
- **Style**: How do they wish to be perceived? (e.g., The Implacable Crusader, The Rigorous Jurist, The Quiet Bureaucrat, The Institutional Modernizer).
- **Motivation**: What drives their energy? (Career ascent to Supreme Court / Fiscal Nacional, peer acclaim, ideological convictions, institutional defense).
- **Integrity**: How rigid is their ethical code when pressured by political masters or public outcry?
- **Competence**: Are they technically sharp in the courtroom, or are they primarily political-bureaucratic operators?
- **Environment**: How does their geographic/institutional setting shape their decisions?

### C. Crisis & Pressure Response
- How does the subject react when:
  1. A case suffers an appellate reversal or collapse.
  2. The media launches aggressive critical scrutiny.
  3. Direct superiors or political authorities demand accountability.
- Observable patterns: defensive stonewalling, aggressive press counter-briefings, deflection to subordinate investigators, or quiet institutional retreat.

### D. Operating Doctrine & Precedents
- Specific to legal / prosecutorial targets:
  - **Plea Bargaining & Abbreviated Procedures**: Are they willing to settle cases efficiently, or do they push relentlessly for oral trials?
  - **Coercive Measures**: What is their baseline threshold for requesting pre-trial detention (prisión preventiva)?
  - **Relationship with Police/Investigative Agencies**: Do they direct police forces (e.g., PDI, Carabineros) with hands-on micromanagement, or do they grant broad investigative autonomy?

## 5. Network & Link Analysis (Sociogram)

Profile targets not in isolation, but embedded in their relational cluster:
1. **Patrons & Mentors**: Who sponsored their promotions, supported their candidacies, or taught them during formative years?
2. **Inner Circle & Trusted Lieutenants**: Who are their go-to assistant prosecutors, advisors, or chiefs of staff who move with them across postings?
3. **Institutional Faction**: Do they align with conservative, reformist, trade-unionist, or corporatist factions within the institution?
4. **Adversarial Nodes**: Are there known ongoing rivalries with specific judges, regional defense attorneys, political figures, or rival prosecutors?

---

## 6. Vulnerabilities, Blindspots & Friction Vectors

Identify the friction points that define the subject's operational exposure:
- **Case Reversals & Judicial Rebuke**: Instances where appellate courts or higher tribunals reprimanded their investigations for procedural violations or lack of evidence.
- **Conflicts of Interest & Disqualifications**: Family ties to law firms, corporate interests, or historical recusal requests.
- **Audit Findings & Administrative Sanctions**: Disciplinary records, Comptroller General (Contraloría) observations, or internal affairs investigations.
- **Media Exposure Points**: Public controversies or soundbites that have created enduring reputational vulnerability.
